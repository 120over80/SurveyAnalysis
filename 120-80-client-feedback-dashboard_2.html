Client feedback: what drives a high score

120/80 GROUP relationship assessment · 2025 and 2026 · 59 responses across 35 client accounts

The short answer

Which qualities correlate with a high score. Four findings, both years agree.

  1. Strategy has the largest effect on the score. In 2026, clients who identified strategic thinking as a strength averaged 9.1. Clients who asked us to improve it averaged 7.2. That 1.9-point difference is the largest of any quality, and 2025 shows the same pattern.
  2. Proactivity is our weakest quality and the one most closely linked to loyalty. Of the three qualities clients rate directly, proactivity scores lowest in both years, 7.8 then 7.9. It also lines up most closely with whether a client says they would recommend us: in 2026 the ten clients who rated our proactivity 9 or 10 gave us a perfect 10 on recommending us, while the three who rated it 6 or below gave an average of 6.0. Understanding of their business and our tools follow the same direction but less tightly, meaning a client can rate those two highly and still stop short of recommending us. Section 1 breaks this down.
  3. Client service is expected rather than distinguishing. Around 90% of clients identify it as a strength regardless of the score they give us, and almost nobody asks us to improve it. It protects the relationship but it does not raise the score.
  4. Low and high raters are asking for different things. Low raters want strategy and results. High raters, when they ask for anything, want measurement and sharper writing. Two different conversations.

Section 1

Which qualities drive the score

Two ways of asking the same question. Both point to strategy and proactivity.

How closely each quality tracks whether a client would recommend us

How to read this. Every client gave us a 1-to-10 rating on each quality, and separately a 1-to-10 answer to "would you recommend us." Grouping clients by the rating they gave a quality shows what those same clients said about recommending us. A quality where the low group and the high group are far apart is a quality that moves loyalty. One where both groups answer about the same does not.

Correlation, side by side with how well we perform

Two different scales. The left column is a correlation, which runs from 0 to 1: 0 means the two answers have no relationship, 1 means they move in perfect step. The right column is an average rating out of 10, straight from the survey. High correlation with a low rating is the combination worth acting on.

What the qualities mean. Proactivity is the survey question on surfacing new opportunities and ideas and up-levelling existing work. Business understanding is the question on how well the team understands the client's business. Tools and processes is the question on whether we use good systems to communicate and manage work.

Score difference by quality

What the plus and minus mean. These are not survey answers. For each quality we take the average overall score of the clients who checked it, then subtract the average score of everyone who did not. A positive number means clients who value that quality score us higher than average. A negative number means clients asking us to improve it score us lower than average. The further from zero, the more that quality separates a happy client from an unhappy one. Qualities with fewer than two clients on either side are left out.

Section 5

What to do about it

Five moves the data supports. Each one names the number behind it.

1. Build a strategy moment into every retainer

Strategy produces the widest score difference we measure: +1.9 for clients who identify it as a strength, −1.9 for clients who ask for more of it. Nobody in the high group asked for better strategy. A standing quarterly session where we bring the recommendation rather than take the brief is the most efficient way to move a passive client into a promoter. Three clients raised this unprompted: Machinify wrote "we're pulling work out of you rather than having you drive us," Infinitus wrote "most of the strategy is coming from our team," and epocrates wrote they are not getting "the same proactive coverage and ideation."

2. Manage proactivity as a tracked metric

It is our lowest rated quality two years running, 7.8 then 7.9, and it aligns with the recommend score more closely than business understanding or tools do. Every other rated question improved in 2026. This one did not. If one metric belongs on a monthly review, this is it.

3. Lead renewal conversations with results, not service

93% of clients identify client service as a strength and almost none ask us to improve it, so it no longer distinguishes us. Results is the opposite: 39% want it stronger, and it produces the second largest score difference. Renewal conversations should open with the coverage and pipeline story rather than responsiveness.

4. Give measurement to the clients who are already satisfied

Measurement is the one area high raters request more often than low raters: 42% of the high group flagged it against 25% of the low group. These are the clients defending our budget internally. COTA stated it directly, asking how to measure impact "in an evidence based way" to support the case for expanding services. Stronger reporting here protects revenue rather than recovering it.

5. Address the four low accounts individually

The 2026 low group is four responses and their reasons differ. Infinitus and epocrates want strategic ownership. Optura wants results and clearer scope. Lantern wants proactive ideas and analytics. Averaging them obscures the fix. Each needs its own conversation.

Worth a closer look

Problem-solving behaves like a threshold quality. Clients who identify it as a strength score only modestly above average, but the three who asked us to improve it score 1.4 points below average. It appears to cost us more when it is missing than it gains us when it is present, which puts it closer to strategy than to writing quality in terms of priority.

A note on sample size. 28 responses in 2026 and 31 in 2025 is a small base, and the low group is four people in each year. Everything here is directional. The patterns are worth acting on because both years point the same way, not because any individual percentage is precise. Raising the response rate above 45% would improve next year's analysis more than any new question would.
